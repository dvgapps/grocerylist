Privacy policy for GroceryList

Effective October 24, 2026

GroceryList is an Android app made by DVGApps. This page explains what the app does with your information. In short: I do not collect, receive or store your data. The app has no accounts, no ads and no analytics of its own. Your data is stored on your phone, and, if you have Google’s backup turned on, Android may also copy it to your own Google account (see Backups). A few optional features contact outside services, and those are listed below so you can decide whether to use them.

Who is responsible

DVGApps (Diedrich Guenther), an independent developer. Questions about this policy: dvgapps.support@gmail.com.

Where your data is stored

Everything you enter, including items, prices, photos, stores, aisle layouts, lists, sets and settings, is stored on your phone in the app’s private storage. I never receive it, and I run no server for this app. If Google’s backup is on, Android may also keep a copy in your own Google account (see Backups).

Outside services the app contacts, only when you use a feature

These requests happen only when you use the feature named. They go straight from your phone to the service, not through me. Like any internet request, the service can see your phone's IP address. Requests to Open Food Facts and Nominatim also carry the app's name and version (GroceryListApp/1.0), which those services ask every app to send.

FeatureServiceWhat is sent
Scanning a barcode in the item editorOpen Food FactsThe barcode number only. The app then downloads the product's name, brand, size and photo, if the database has them, and saves them on your phone.

I do not control these services and cannot say what they do with the requests they receive. Follow the links for their policies. If you never scan a barcode, search for an address or open a map, the app makes none of these requests.

Location

Location is optional and off unless you turn on “Auto-select nearby store” or tap “use my current location” for a store. The app reads your position through Android, compares it with the store positions saved on your phone, and does not send it anywhere. The coordinates of stores you save are kept in the app’s database on your phone, and are included in any backup of the app’s data (see Backups). Your phone's own location services are part of Android and are governed by Google's and your device maker's terms.

Camera and photos

To add a photo to an item, the app opens your phone's camera app or photo picker. The photo you choose is copied into the app's private storage. The app does not request camera or storage permission.

Backups

Backups you make

In Settings → Database Management, “Backup Now” saves a file to a location that you choose, and “Restore from a backup” reads a file you choose. The app never uploads a backup anywhere on its own. Where you keep the file is up to you.

Android’s automatic backup

If Google’s backup is switched on in your phone’s settings, Android may copy the app’s data, including photos, to your own Google account. If you reinstall the app or set up a new phone, Android can restore that copy. This is done by the operating system, not by me, and I do not receive it. It is governed by Google’s terms and your Google account settings, and you can turn it off or manage it in your phone’s backup settings. A backup file saved with “Backup Now” is still the surest way to get your items, stores and lists back.

What I do not do

  • I do not collect personal information, and I have no accounts or sign-in.
  • I do not show ads and do not use advertising IDs.
  • I do not use analytics or crash-reporting services in the app.
  • I do not sell or share your data. I do not have it.

Children

GroceryList is a general-purpose app and is not directed at children. I do not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Your rights

Because I hold none of your data, there is nothing for me to access, correct or delete. You control your data: you can remove items inside the app and use “Reset all data to stock” in Settings → Database Management. Uninstalling the app removes its data from your phone, but if Google’s backup is on, a copy may remain in your Google account and can come back if you reinstall. To remove it, manage or delete the app’s backup in your phone’s Google backup settings. Backup files you saved yourself are yours to delete.

Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, for example when purchases are added, I will update this page and its date before the change reaches you.
